Fair

This is a mature, disciplined PRD: FR/consequence coupling is close to exhaustive (no vague adjectives found anywhere in 55 FRs), trade-offs are named with what was given up, and the single-persona/light-UJ shape matches a solo developer-owner tool built for downstream AI-agent consumption. What the rubric flags as the main risk is not the PRD's own prose but its two traceability companions falling behind: .decision-log.md stopped at 2026-06-21 and never logged the Release 3/4 decisions (FR-44 through FR-55) the PRD narrates inline, and SPEC.md — declared canonical in §0 — still only covers Release 1/2 and is silent on everything shipped since.

The adversarial pass materially shifts the picture from "traceability lag" to "the drift has produced real content defects." It surfaces a genuine required/optional contradiction on the one field FR-11/FR-13 call load-bearing (contract start date), a specification bug where FR-37's euro-denominated budget alert reintroduces the exact Tariff-price noise FR-14 was explicitly designed to avoid, and at least two shipped, user-facing behaviors (the Epic 9 meter-reset chart indicator, decimal-precision input rejection) with zero corresponding FR — meaning the PRD's own claim to be the "single source of requirements truth" is measurably false for parts of the shipped product. None of this rises to a broken dimension or a critical finding on its own, but combined with the rubric's traceability findings it argues for a dedicated reconciliation pass before this PRD is trusted as an unread source of truth for a new repo.

Adversarial review

review-adversarial-general.md

A cynical pass assuming problems exist, focused on internal inconsistencies, unstated assumptions, and places where the PRD reads as written for an earlier, simpler version of the product than 13 epics of iteration would produce. 15 findings total; the 12 below are distinct from the rubric's own findings (three others independently corroborated rubric findings above and are noted inline there instead of repeated).

high

Contract start date required/optional contradiction

FR-4, FR-6 vs FR-10, §4.4, FR-11, FR-13
FR-4 and FR-6 list the Onboarding Tariff's contract start date as optional. §4.4 and FR-10 state every Tariff entry has a required contract start date, and FR-11/FR-13 depend on it as the sole anchor for cost-period resolution and price locking. The PRD never resolves what happens for FR-11/FR-13 if the Onboarding Tariff is saved without one.
Fix: Align FR-4/FR-6 with FR-10's "required" rule, or if genuinely optional at onboarding, add a Consequences clause to FR-11/FR-13 defining fallback behavior when the field is absent.
high

Budget Pressure Alert reintroduces the Tariff-noise problem FR-14 was designed around

FR-37 vs FR-14
FR-14 anchors the Dashboard's budget delta to kWh instead of euros specifically because "euro figures shift with every Tariff change." FR-37 compares a euro-denominated rolling cost projection against a static euro "planned annual spend" — so a mid-year Tariff price increase alone, with flat or falling consumption, can fire a false budget-pressure alert. FR-43 correctly anchors to kWh; FR-37 doesn't, and the inconsistency is never acknowledged.
Fix: Anchor FR-37 to a kWh-vs-baseline comparison consistent with FR-14/FR-43, or explicitly state why FR-37 accepts the euro-noise trade-off FR-14 was written to avoid.
high

Shipped meter-reset chart indicator has no corresponding FR

Epic 9 Story 9.8 vs FR-16/FR-17
Epic 9 Story 9.8 ("Meter Reset Visual Indicator") shipped a third trend-chart visual state (a distinct hatched/reset-icon bar wherever a reading drops below the prior one). FR-16/FR-17 still describe only two states (normal vs. spike), so the PRD's own account of the trend chart is stale relative to the product it claims to specify.
Fix: Add an FR (or amend FR-16/FR-17) documenting the meter-reset visual state and its trigger condition.
medium

Decision-log citations resolve to unrelated entries

§10 (Q-3/Q-4/Q-6) vs .decision-log.md D-13/D-17/D-18
§10 annotates Q-3/Q-4/Q-6 with "(UX D-13)," "(UX D-17)," "(UX D-18)" — but the log's actual D-13/D-17/D-18 entries cover unrelated topics (Eve Home timestamp handling, locale-formats.md authority, §1 Vision framing). The real matching entries appear to be D-22/D-23/D-24; two documents evidently reuse "D-N" numbering for unrelated decisions with no disambiguation.
Fix: Correct §10's citations to the actual matching decision-log IDs, and disambiguate the numbering scheme if two logs share the "D-N" convention.
medium

Undocumented decimal-precision input validation

Epic 9 Stories 9.7/9.11 vs FR-8, FR-24–31
The shipped system rejects kWh submissions with excess decimal precision (per Epic 9's shared validator extension method and its regression test), but no FR anywhere specifies any precision or decimal-place constraint on numeric kWh input — a real, user-facing rejection path with zero requirements-level grounding.
Fix: Add a Consequences clause to FR-8 (or a new FR) specifying the accepted decimal precision for kWh input and the rejection behavior.
medium

Reconciliation invariant asserted as fact, not a handled case

FR-27 vs §1, FR-33
"Attributed kWh across all plugs does not exceed the Main Meter total for any period" is stated unconditionally, but measurement error can make smart plugs collectively over-report relative to the meter, producing a mathematically negative Residual. The PRD never states what happens in that case, despite the Residual being one of only three named design principles and "always shown, never suppressed."
Fix: Add a Consequences clause to FR-27/FR-33 specifying handling of over-reporting (clamp to zero, flag as a measurement-error state, or surface a data-quality warning).
medium

"status: final" doesn't reflect four rounds of post-finalization amendment

frontmatter vs §6 (Release 3/4, two same-day correct-course insertions)
Frontmatter has said "final" since 2026-06-20, yet §6 documents Release 3 "added post-Epic-7 retro," Release 4 "added 2026-08-01 via architecture review + brainstorming session," plus two same-day bmad-correct-course insertions for FR-54 and FR-55 — at least four rounds of scope change with no status field ever reflecting continuous amendment.
Fix: Update frontmatter to reflect the document's actual living status, or log each post-finalization change as a formal revision with its own updated timestamp.
medium

No-notification spike detection sits against the product's core emotional promise

FR-17 vs §2.1, §1
FR-17 explicitly generates "No separate banner or notification" for a spike — visible only as a chart color change the user must proactively open Insights to see. This sits against the stated emotional JTBD ("Stop being surprised by my annual energy invoice") and the Vision's promise to know about a surprising invoice before it arrives. A silent spike is exactly the kind of event most likely to cause a surprise invoice, with no rationale given for excluding it from the notification-worthy Insight types.
Fix: Either add a lightweight, non-intrusive spike notification, or state explicitly in FR-17 why silent chart-only encoding is intentional despite the "no surprise invoice" promise.
medium

Date-aware attribution vs. interpolation interaction is unspecified

FR-52/FR-53 vs FR-26
FR-52 gates Decomposition inclusion by InUseSince/DecommissionedDate, and FR-53 splits a reassigned Device's consumption by Room per day — but neither FR states whether this date-gating happens before or after a gap is filled by linear interpolation, nor how an interpolated day spanning a reassignment boundary should be split.
Fix: Add a Consequences clause specifying the ordering (e.g., "interpolated values are attributed to whichever Room/Device is active on that calendar day per FR-52's InUseSince window").
low

"Hub-free" principle is literally false of the product as worded

§1
§1 defines hub-free as including "no cloud subscription," yet the entire product runs on paid Azure services (NFR-4 even prices Azure SQL at ~€5/month). The principle presumably means "no smart-hub-vendor cloud subscription," but the PRD never disambiguates this from the app's own Azure hosting.
Fix: Reword the §1 principle to explicitly exclude the app's own hosting from the claim (e.g., "no smart-hub-vendor cloud subscription").
low

Household-size kWh presets are uncited and inconsistently scaled

FR-5
Unlike every other numeric constant in the document (all traced to a decision-log entry, SPEC.md, or an architecture/brainstorming session), FR-5's 1,500/2,500/3,500/4,250 kWh presets for 1-4 persons have no citation, and the progression itself is uneven (+1,000, +1,000, then only +750).
Fix: Cite a source for the presets, or mark them explicitly as a rough placeholder assumption in §11.
low

Multi-user architecture asserted with no FR ever exercising it

§9, NFR-2, §1 vs §2.2, §5
§9/NFR-2 describe genuine per-user tenant isolation and §1 promises the architecture will accommodate "additional users" eventually, yet there is no FR anywhere for user registration, invitation, or account provisioning, and §2.2/§5 confirm there's no admin or multi-user UI. After 13 shipped epics of single-owner deployment, this reads as unexercised aspiration carried over from an early draft.
Fix: Either add a placeholder FR/Non-Goal explicitly deferring multi-user to a named future release, or drop the "additional users" promise from §1 if no plan exists.

Mechanical notes

  • Terminology drift: "v1/v2" vs "Release 1-4." §2.2, §5, §9, and §11's [A-2] still use pre-Release-3/4 "v1/v2" vocabulary; a reader can't tell if these Non-Goals are scoped to the first two releases or everything shipped to date. Independently flagged by both reviewers. Recommend normalizing to "Release 1-4" or "current scope" throughout.
  • Glossary case/term consistency is otherwise clean — capitalized defined terms are used identically across FRs, UJs, and SM definitions.
  • ID continuity confirmed: FR-1 through FR-55 present exactly once each (55 unique IDs, no gaps or duplicates) despite non-sequential addition order.
  • Assumptions Index roundtrip: no inline [ASSUMPTION: ...] tags remain in the PRD body; §11 now functions as a historical resolution record rather than a live roundtrip-checkable index.
  • Epics without FR mapping (Epic 9, Epic 11, Epic 13 — engineering-hardening/bugfix work) are, by design, absent from §6's Release table. Consistent with project precedent, but a reader of the PRD alone has no way to know these shipped epics exist.
  • The SPEC-governs-on-conflict rule (§0) has no maintained tripwire — no mechanism anywhere detects when SPEC.md and the PRD actually diverge, which is how the Release 3/4 staleness above went unnoticed. Corroborated by the adversarial pass.
